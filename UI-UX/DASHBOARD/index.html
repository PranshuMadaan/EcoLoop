The problem

The people who recycle India’s waste have no record.

India produces about 62 million tonnes of municipal waste a year. Official figures say most of it is now collected, but how much is truly processed is disputed, and Delhi alone still sends over 4,000 tonnes a day to dumpsites. Informal pickers recover much of what is recycled, yet many earn only ₹300–500 a day.

They sell to dealers at prices they can’t check, with no ID, no weight record and no credit history. Brands can’t prove EPR compliance either, because the chain runs on cash and memory.

What EcoLoop does

It makes the chain visible, then fair.

Every picker gets a verified digital ID and a weight log. Every recycler can post live buy orders in ₹/kg. Every collection truck follows a route based on how full the bins are. Payouts reach the picker by UPI, with receipts in their own language on WhatsApp.

Who it helps

Everyone in the loop gains something real.

Pickers earn more and gain a formal identity. Households get rewarded for sorting at source. Cities cut fuel and tipping fees. Recyclers get cleaner, graded material. Brands get an audit trail for EPR credits.

Why choose it

It works alongside what already exists.

EcoLoop runs next to existing municipal fleets and builds on the co-operative models of SWaCH Pune and Hasiru Dala, so no one is displaced. It supports the four-stream segregation required by the SWM Rules 2026 and the CPCB EPR portal, and aims to earn its own revenue instead of depending on subsidies.

Revenue comes from a 1.5–2.5% marketplace commission, a per-tonne fee for verified traceability data, municipal analytics subscriptions, and organic waste supply to bio-CNG plants. EPR certificate prices are market-driven (reports cite roughly ₹870–1,500 per tonne for some plastic categories), so our data fee must stay a small share of that. SATAT’s ₹46/kg is a minimum price paid to CBG plants, not to waste suppliers.

Built for how India really works.

We checked our major claims against official and field sources. Here is what we found and how the design responds.

QuestionWhat we foundHow EcoLoop responds
RulesSWM Rules 2026 took effect on 1 April 2026 and require four-stream segregation: wet, dry, sanitary and special care.Batches are tagged by all four streams.
EPRCertificates are generated by registered recyclers on the CPCB portal, and trading is portal-only.We supply verified collection data. We never issue or sell certificates.
Picker accessAbout 6 in 10 pickers have a bank account, few use digital payments, and over 60% have no formal education.Voice-first WhatsApp, assisted sign-up at hubs, and hub cash-out when UPI isn’t possible.
City partnersSWaCH saves Pune an estimated ₹113 Cr a year, yet reports say Pune began replacing doorstep collection with mechanised vehicles in 2025. Bengaluru’s dry waste centres have faced payment delays.Start with one ward under a written agreement, and earn revenue from recyclers and brands so we don’t rely only on city payments.
CompetitionRecykal already runs a marketplace with 600+ recyclers and 5,000+ aggregators.Focus on the picker-level ledger and dry waste centre grading, and stay open to integrating with existing platforms.
Income gainsComparable programmes report 20–40% higher earnings.We plan for 25–40% and treat 2.3× as a stretch goal.
Route savingsTypical savings are 15–25% of fleet distance, and live fill levels need sensors most wards lack.Start with driver and supervisor fill reports, then add sensors.
Data privacyConsent, security and breach-reporting duties under the DPDP Rules become enforceable on 13 May 2027.Collect minimum data, use OTP-based IDs and ask for clear consent from day one.
